Subject: Summary of Handheld vs. Standard Care POCUS Analysis (Early Pregnancy & Gynaecology)

Dear Stakeholder Team,

We have completed the comprehensive analysis comparing Handheld (HH) Point-of-Care Ultrasound against Standard Care (SC) machines. Our evaluation covers clinical measurements, diagnostic consistency, demographic factors, and advanced radiomics analysis across both Early Pregnancy (EP) and Gynaecology cohorts.

Here are the key findings from our analysis:

### 1. Early Pregnancy (EP) Performance: High Agreement
Our analysis indicates robust agreement between HH and SC in early pregnancy assessments:
- **Detection Rates:** Excellent concordance in detecting fundamental structures (Gestational Sac, Yolk Sac, Fetal Pole, Cardiac Activity).
- **Measurements:** Continuous measurements (CRL, Gestational Sac size, Endometrial Thickness) show no clinically significant differences.
- **Clinical Management:** Remarkable consistency, with extremely high agreement in final diagnosis and management plans between HH and SC.

### 2. Gynaecology Performance: Consistency Challenges
While basic measurements align well, gynaecological assessments present more challenges for HH devices:
- **Measurements:** Uterine dimensions, endometrial thickness, and ovarian volumes show a high level of agreement.
- **Data Completeness:** Handheld devices capture significantly fewer data fields per patient compared to Standard Care (p < 0.001).
- **Management Consistency:** There is a notable drop in management consistency in gynaecological cases, which underscores the complexity of full pelvic workflows on HH devices.

### 3. The Impact of BMI on Image Reliability
We observed a critical limitation of handheld devices related to patient Body Mass Index (BMI):
- Across measurement categories (CRL, GS, Endometrium, Uterus, Ovaries), there is a statistically negative correlation between increasing BMI and measurement concordance. In heavier patients, HH reliability drops notably, highlighting the physical limitations of current handheld probes in penetrating deeper tissue.

### 4. Advanced Analysis (Radiomics)
We conducted a quantitative radiomics analysis to assess textural and pixel-level consistency between HH and SC images:
- **Correlations:** Both image-level and patient-level radiomic features exhibit very low correlation.
- **Conclusion:** Whole-image textural features from HH devices do not reliably match those from SC machines due to differences in image processing, UI overlay, and depth. Investigating Region of Interest (ROI)-based radiomics (focusing solely on the gestational sac or endometrium rather than the whole screen) may yield more robust comparisons in the future.

### Next Steps and Summary Recommendations
1. **Clinical Recommendation:** Handheld devices are highly effective, reliable, and safe for early pregnancy triage and basic biometry. However, standard care machines remain necessary for comprehensive gynaecological workups and for patients with higher BMI where handheld reliability decreases.
2. **Further Research:** We will pivot our radiomics analysis towards targeted ROI extraction to better capture structural differences without the interference of background noise and UI elements.

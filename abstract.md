## Abstract: Comparative Performance of Handheld vs. Standard Care Point-of-Care Ultrasound in Early Pregnancy and Gynaecology

**Objective:** To evaluate the clinical concordance, diagnostic reliability, and textural similarity between Handheld (HH) Point-of-Care Ultrasound devices and Standard Care (SC) machines in early pregnancy and gynaecological assessments.

**Methods:** We conducted a retrospective comparative analysis of two patient cohorts: Early Pregnancy (EP) (n=229) and Gynaecology (n=218). We assessed clinical measurement concordance, data completeness, and diagnostic management agreement between HH and SC modalities. The impact of patient Body Mass Index (BMI) on measurement reliability was quantified using continuous correlation. Additionally, a quantitative radiomics analysis extracted and compared texture features from paired HH and SC images to assess pixel-level similarity.

**Results:** In the Early Pregnancy cohort (n=229, mean age 33.6, mean gestational age 7.3 weeks), HH devices demonstrated robust clinical utility, achieving 92.5% (211/228) diagnostic agreement with SC machines. Conversely, the Gynaecology cohort (n=218, mean age 44.2) showed poor management agreement between devices at only 40.3% (87/216), despite continuous clinical biometric measurements showing no statistically significant differences between modalities (e.g., mean endometrial thickness: HH 5.74 mm vs. SC 5.59 mm, p=0.941; mean uterine length: HH 61.93 mm vs. SC 63.72 mm, p=0.707). In both cohorts, data completeness was significantly lower on HH devices compared to SC (EP: mean 18.59 vs. 34.44 fields/patient, p<0.001; Gynaecology: mean 21.22 vs. 29.92 fields/patient, p<0.001). 

Correlation analyses identified patient BMI as a limiting factor; increasing BMI demonstrated a trend toward greater absolute differences in clinical measurements (e.g., EP Mean Sac Diameter disparity, r=0.113, p=0.173, n=146), reflecting the depth penetration limitations of handheld probes. Furthermore, advanced radiomics analysis of paired images (n=13 patients) revealed low and predominantly negative feature correlation between devices (Uniformity r=-0.51; Entropy r=-0.44), indicating significant disparities in whole-image texture profiles largely driven by hardware variations and proprietary software interpolation.

**Conclusion:** Handheld ultrasound operates as a highly reliable and safe triage tool in early pregnancy, matching Standard Care in diagnostic capabilities. However, its utility is constrained in comprehensive gynaecological assessments and in patients with elevated BMI, where standard machines remain imperative. Given the significant textural discordance between devices, future efforts to deploy AI and radiomics on handheld platforms must prioritize targeted Region of Interest (ROI) extraction over whole-image analysis.
